Question 1 of 5
What type of health insurance do you currently have?
To open an HSA, you must be enrolled in a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). In 2026, all ACA Bronze and Catastrophic plans automatically qualify.
Question 2 of 5
Are you also enrolled in any other health coverage?
Having a second health plan — even a spouse's — can disqualify you if it covers medical expenses before your HDHP deductible is met.
Question 3 of 5
Are you currently enrolled in Medicare?
Once you enroll in any part of Medicare (Part A, B, C, or D), you can no longer contribute to an HSA. You can still use existing HSA funds.
Question 4 of 5
Can someone claim you as a dependent on their tax return?
If another person — like a parent — can claim you as a dependent on their taxes, you're not eligible to contribute to your own HSA.

Other tax-advantaged options to consider

If your employer offers an FSA, you can save up to $3,200 pre-tax for medical expenses in 2026. Unlike an HSA, it doesn't require an HDHP.

You might be eligible — but need to verify one thing

Your answers put you in a gray area. Here's exactly what to check before opening an HSA.

What to verify before opening an HSA
📄
Check your plan documents. Look for the words "HDHP" or "High Deductible Health Plan" in your Summary of Benefits. The 2026 minimum individual deductible must be at least $1,650.
💑
Confirm your spouse's plan type. If you're on your spouse's plan, verify it's also an HDHP. A non-HDHP spousal plan covering medical expenses before your deductible disqualifies you.
📅
Check your Medicare enrollment date. If you're turning 65 this year, you can contribute to your HSA up until the month you enroll in Medicare — timing your enrollment carefully can save thousands.
🧾
Check with your tax preparer. If you receive any VA benefits, check whether they cover non-service-related conditions — this can affect eligibility.
